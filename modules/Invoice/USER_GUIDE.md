# Invoices – User Guide

Invoices are the billing records used to request payment for delivered products or services. An invoice can be linked to an organization, contact, opportunity, Sales Order, and quote and contains the items and amounts being billed.

## What you can do

- Create an invoice directly or generate it from a Sales Order, Quote, or Opportunity.
- Add products and services with quantities, prices, discounts, and taxes.
- Record the invoice date, due date, customer reference, purchase order number, and payment terms.
- Distinguish a regular invoice from a **Final Invoice** or **Credit Invoice** where those options are available.
- Track payment progress with statuses such as **Created**, **Approved**, **Sent**, and **Paid**.
- Review the amount received and the remaining balance.
- Export the invoice to PDF or send it by email with the PDF attached.
- Create a Purchase Order from the invoice when the required permissions and module are available.

## Typical workflow

1. Generate an invoice from the related Sales Order or create it from the Invoices module.
2. Confirm the customer, source documents, invoice date, due date, and payment terms.
3. Verify all line items, discounts, taxes, currency, and the grand total.
4. Save the invoice and move it through approval and sending.
5. Send the PDF to the customer.
6. When payment is received, record the received amount and update the status to **Paid** when appropriate.

## Amounts and payment tracking

The invoice summary shows the subtotal, discounts, taxes, grand total, received amount, and balance. The balance is the amount still outstanding, so check it after every payment update. Use the invoice's currency and payment terms consistently with the source Sales Order or Quote.

## Helpful tips

- Check the due date before sending the invoice.
- Confirm that the invoice contains the correct source Sales Order and customer reference.
- Use **Sent** to distinguish an invoice issued to the customer from one that is only prepared internally.
- Do not delete an incorrect invoice when your accounting process requires a traceable correction; use the appropriate credit or corrected invoice process.
- Preview the PDF before sending it externally.
